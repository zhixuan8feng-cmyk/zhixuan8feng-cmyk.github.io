I am a 5th-year Ph.D. candidate in Economics at North Carolina State University, co-advised by Denis Pelletier. I will be on the 2026–2027 academic job market and am available for interviews.

My research focuses on financial econometrics, high-frequency trading data, and applied time series. I am primarily interested in understanding how cross-asset dependencies evolve under market frictions and structural shifts, as well as developing scalable econometric frameworks for high-dimensional volatility and covariance forecasting.

In my dissertation, I study the dynamics of high-dimensional realized covariance matrices constructed from tick-level data using multivariate realized kernels. Part of my work investigates the trade-offs between model complexity and generalizability—contrasting heterogeneous autoregressive (HAR) and regularized VAR specifications (via Lasso, Ridge, and Elastic Net) in large asset spaces. Building on this, I develop a block-constrained Regime-Switching Multivariate Realized GARCH (RS-MRG) framework that couples intraday realized measures with standardized return innovations. By projecting covariance structures onto industry equivalence classes and estimating latent state transitions via the EM algorithm and Hamilton filter, this approach overcomes the curse of dimensionality, uncovers state-dependent asymmetric contagion during turbulent market regimes, and substantially mitigates out-of-sample portfolio risk.
