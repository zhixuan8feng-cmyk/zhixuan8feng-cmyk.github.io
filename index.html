About Me

I am a 5th-year Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Economics at North Carolina State University. I am on the 2026–2027 academic job market and available for interviews.

My research lies in financial econometrics, high-frequency trading data, and time series analysis. Specifically, my dissertation develops regime-switching and block-constrained Hidden Markov Models for high-dimensional realized covariance matrices within Realized GARCH frameworks.

Research

High-Dimensional Covariance Dynamics & Latent State Inference (Job Market Paper)
with Denis Pelletier
This paper develops a two-stage Hidden Markov Model with a block-structure specification to capture structural shifts in return-volatility dynamics while alleviating the curse of dimensionality. Daily positive semi-definite covariance matrices are constructed using refreshing-time and multivariate realized kernels, with the Hamilton Filter and Louis Formula implemented for recursive state inference and robust parameter estimation.

Regime-Switching in Multivariate Realized GARCH: A Block-Constrained Hidden Markov Approach (Working Paper)
with Denis Pelletier
